## The corridor

- Port Aransas has exactly **two** land accesses: the Aransas Pass ferry, and SH 361 south down Mustang Island.
- TxDOT project limits: **Access Road 1 (Port Aransas) → Park Road 22**, described by TxDOT as an **approximate 15-mile corridor**. (Winston's framing of "17–18 miles" is the fuller Port A ↔ PR 22 drive; use TxDOT's 15 miles when citing the project.)
- TxDOT project **CSJ: 2263-03-024**, Nueces County. Corpus Christi District.
- Traffic is overwhelmingly **destinational** — no port, distribution, or industrial generators on the stretch. This is the anomaly the piece rests on: the usual explanation for a high-crash rural highway (commercial/truck through-traffic) is absent.

## The record — crashes and deaths

**From TxDOT crash records, via KRIS/KIII reporting:**
- **17 fatalities** on Highway 361 between Port Aransas and Padre Island over ~10 years. (An earlier KIII piece cited a crash as the **15th** fatality of the decade — the count climbed during the reporting window; useful for showing accumulation over time.)
- **Nearly 900 crashes** over the same ~10-year period.
- TxDOT's own traffic-safety crash analysis ranked the **top 5 contributing factors**:
  1. Failure to control speed
  2. **Failure to yield right of way — turning left** ← access/turning conflict
  3. Failure to drive in a single lane
  4. **Failure to yield right of way — stop sign** ← access/intersection conflict
  5. Driving under the influence

  → **Two of TxDOT's own top five are turning/access-point failures.** This is the strongest available support for the developments thesis and it comes from the agency itself.

**From the City of Corpus Christi, _Padre/Mustang Island Mobility Plan — Existing Conditions Memorandum_ (primary document; pulled from TxDOT CRIS, Jan 2018 – Dec 2022):**
- **931 crashes** reported in the Study Area over five years.
- **411 crashes (44%) were associated with driveways or intersections.**
- **6 fatal crashes** in the Study Area in that five-year window; plus 4 more involving pedestrians and 6 involving bicyclists.
- Top contributing factor: **speeding**, followed by **failure to yield right-of-way**.
- **Upward trend since 2019.**
- Crashes rise from **March (Spring Break) through August**.
- **Crash density is highest along PR 22 and Whitecap Blvd.**

⚠️ **GEOGRAPHY CAVEAT — do not misattribute.** The 931 / 411 / 44% figures cover the whole Padre/Mustang **Study Area**, NOT the SH 361 Mustang Island stretch alone. Crash density peaks on the Padre Island side (PR 22, Whitecap). Citing 44% as an SH 361 figure would be a records-floor violation.
→ *But note the analytical value:* the segment with the **most** development and access points (PR 22 / Whitecap) has the **highest** crash density. That is the corridor's own natural experiment, and it is the future SH 361 is being built toward.

## The timeline — diagnosis vs. shovel

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2018** | **Access Management Study** conducted, corridors included **SH 361 (PR 22 → Zahn Rd)**; recommended **directional medians** and shared-use path. Access problem formally identified. |
| 2023 | TxDOT preliminary data collection |
| Fall 2024 | Public Meeting #1 |
| Fall 2024 – Fall 2025 | Preliminary design + environmental studies |
| Summer 2025 | Public Meeting #2 |
| 2026 | Public hearing + environmental clearance |
| 2026–2029 | Final design, ROW acquisition, utility relocation |
| **2030** | **Begin construction** — "*Project advancement is contingent upon results of environmental process and funding availability*" |

→ **~12 years from access-management diagnosis (2018) to first shovel (2030), if funding holds.** Rep. Todd Hunter has pushed to accelerate funding.

## The proposed fix is itself access management

TxDOT's proposal: widen to **four lanes** (two each direction), **raised center medians with left-turn lanes as needed**, and a sidewalk/shared-use path. A raised median's stated purpose is to **cut down head-on crashes**.
→ The remedy TxDOT selected is precisely the remedy for turning-movement conflict. The agency has effectively conceded the diagnosis; the argument is about the 2030 date, not the physics.

## THESIS DISCIPLINE (the honest version)

The vulnerable version: *"developments are causing the crashes."* TxDOT's #1 factor is speeding and DUI is in the top five — a piece ignoring that gets discredited.

The defensible version, which the records actually support:
> A two-lane undivided highway built as a **conduit** — a beginning and an end — is being converted into a road with **driveways**, without the access-management engineering that a road with driveways requires. Driver error (speed, alcohol) is constant everywhere; what a corridor's **design** determines is whether an ordinary error becomes a fender-bender or a head-on fatality. Every new access point adds conflict opportunities to a road with no median to absorb them. TxDOT identified this in 2018, selected the correct remedy, and scheduled it for 2030 — contingent on funding.

# ANGLE 2 — The attention gap (PAL-native; South Jetty is the only other outlet with this)

**The contrast Winston is drawing:** while the state artery kills people, the City spends its traffic-engineering attention on in-town intersections.

**Jurisdictional discipline (makes this angle un-swattable):** SH 361 is a **state** highway (TxDOT); Ave G/Station and 6th/Cutoff are **city** streets.
